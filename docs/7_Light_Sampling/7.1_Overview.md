# 7.1 Overview

BSDF sampling alone converges extremely slowly as it must stochastically find light sources. Next Event Estimation, or NEE solves this by directly sampling the light source rather than waiting for a bounce to hit it. At every bounce, NEE fires a *shadow ray* which is ray travelling from the bounce point directly in the direction of the light source. The shadow ray's light contribution depends on its occlusion (if it is blocked from the hitting the light source directly).

Lights are split into two distinct categories: finite lights and infinite lights.

- Finite Lights: Point lights, spotlights, or area lights exist at bounded positions in space
- Infinite Lights: Directional lights, HDRI environment are determined by a direction rather than a position

Because BSDF and NEE sampling can both sample the same light source, it overcontributes the image if you directly add up the contributions. Thus, *multiple importance sampling* (MIS) is used (see [7.6 MIS](7.7_Multiple_Importance_Sampling.md)).

Note: direct sampling already implicitly gives the outgoing light direction. Therefore, special BSDF evaluation functions are implemented separate from the stochastic BSDF sampling such as the one [here](../5_BSDF/5.11_BSDF_Sampling.md).
