# Uniform Disk Sampling

`UniformSampleUnitDisk` generates a uniformly distributed position on a unit disk.

``` GLSL
vec2 UniformSampleUnitDisk(inout uvec3 rng) {
    vec3 Xi = Pcg3d(rng);
    float Xi1 = Xi.x;
    float Xi2 = Xi.y;
    
    float r = sqrt(Xi1);
    float theta = 2.0 * PI * Xi2;
    return vec2(r * cos(theta), r * sin(theta));
}
```

## Derivation

We want to get the cartesian coordinates $(x, y)$ generated from
$2$ independent uniform random numbers $\xi_1, \xi_2 \in [0, 1)$.

The area of a unit disk is $\pi (1)^2 = \pi$.
Thus, the uniform distribution over it is

$$
\operatorname{PDF}_{X, Y}(x, y) = \dfrac{1}{\pi}
$$

To sample in polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$, we convert from $(x, y)$ using the known relations

$$
x = r\cos{\theta}, \quad y = r\sin{\theta}
$$

Changing variables requires the absolute value of the Jacobian determinant $J_{R, \Theta}$.
As such,

$$
\operatorname{PDF}_{R, \Theta}(r, \theta) = \operatorname{PDF}_{X, Y}(x, y) \cdot |J_{R, \Theta}|
$$

We can calculate the Jacobian matrix as follows:

$$
J_{R, \Theta} = \begin{vmatrix}
\frac{\partial{x}}{\partial{r}} & \frac{\partial{x}}{\partial{\theta}} \\
\frac{\partial{y}}{\partial{r}} & \frac{\partial{y}}{\partial{\theta}}
\end{vmatrix} =
\begin{vmatrix}
\cos{\theta} & -r\sin{\theta} \\
\sin{\theta} & r\cos{\theta}
\end{vmatrix} =
r
$$

Since $x = r\cos{\theta}$ and $y = r\sin{\theta}$, computing the partial derivatives gives us

$$
\frac{\partial{x}}{\partial{r}} = \cos{\theta}, \quad
\frac{\partial{x}}{\partial{\theta}} = -r\sin{\theta}, \quad
\frac{\partial{y}}{\partial{r}} = \sin{\theta}, \quad
\frac{\partial{y}}{\partial{\theta}} = r\cos{\theta}
$$

Plugging these into the Jacobian matrix gives

$$
J_{R, \Theta} =
\begin{vmatrix}
\cos{\theta} & -r\sin{\theta} \\
\sin{\theta} & r\cos{\theta}
\end{vmatrix}
$$

Solving,

$$
\begin{align*}
|J_{R, \Theta}| &= (\cos{\theta})(r\cos{\theta}) - (-r\sin{\theta})(\sin{\theta}) \\
&= r\cos^2{\theta} + r\sin^2{\theta} \\
&= r(\cos^2{\theta} + \sin^2{\theta}) \\
&= r
\end{align*}
$$

</details>

Therefore,

$$
\operatorname{PDF}_{R, \Theta}(r, \theta) =
\operatorname{PDF}_{X, Y}(x, y) \cdot |J_{R, \Theta}| =
\dfrac{1}{\pi} \cdot r =
\dfrac{r}{\pi}
$$

We can then solve for the respective marginal and conditional PDFs:

$$
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{PDF}_R(r)
&= \int_0^{2\pi}{\operatorname{PDF}_{R, \Theta}(r, \theta) d{\theta}} \\
&= \int_0^{2\pi}{\dfrac{r}{\pi} d{\theta}} \\
&= 2r
\end{align*} \\

\begin{align*}
\operatorname{PDF}_\Theta(\theta|r)
&= \dfrac{\operatorname{PDF}_{R, \Theta}(r, \theta)}{\operatorname{PDF}_R(r)} \\
&= \dfrac{\frac{r}{\pi}}{2r} \\
&= \dfrac{1}{2\pi}
\end{align*}
$$

Solving for the CDFs, we have

$$
\operatorname{CDF}_R(r) =
\int_0^r{\operatorname{PDF}_R(t)dt} =
\int_0^r 2t = r^2 \\


\operatorname{CDF}_\Theta(\theta) =
\int_0^\theta{\operatorname{PDF}_\Theta(t)dt} =
\int_0^\theta{\dfrac{1}{2\pi}dt} =
\dfrac{\theta}{2\pi}
$$

Using inverse transform sampling, we finally have

$$
\xi_{1} = \operatorname{CDF}_R(r) = r^2
\implies r = \sqrt{\xi_{1}} \\

\xi_{2} = \operatorname{CDF}_\Theta(\theta) = \dfrac{\theta}{2\pi}
\implies \theta = 2\pi\xi_{2}
$$
