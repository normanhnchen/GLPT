# The Rendering Equation

The rendering equation (also known as the light transport equation) is a mathematical formula which describes the radiance arriving at a point.
It states that the outgoing radiance $L_o(\text{p}, \omega_o)$ at point $\text{p}$ in the outgoing light direction $\omega_o$ is the sum of the emitted radiance
$L_e(\text{p}, \omega_o)$ in that direction and the radiance $L_i(\text{p}, \omega_i)$ accumulated from all directions on the hemisphere $\Omega$ multiplied by the BSDF $f(\text{p}, \omega_o, \omega_i)$ and the Lambertian cosine term $|\text{n}\cdot\omega_i|$:

$$
L_o(\text{p}, \omega_o) = L_e(\text{p}, \omega_o) + \int_{\Omega}{f(\text{p}, \omega_o, \omega_i)L_i(\text{p}, \omega_i)|\text{n}\cdot\omega_i|d\omega_i}
$$

Since a path tracer uses stochastic sampling, we can rewrite the rendering equation as a Monte Carlo estimator equation:

$$
L_o(\text{p}, \omega_o) \approx L_e(\text{p}, \omega_o) + \dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^N{\dfrac{f(\text{p}, \omega_o, \omega_j)L_i(\text{p}, \omega_j)|\text{n}\cdot\omega_j|}{\text{PDF}_{\Omega_j}(\omega_j)}}
$$
