# 6.2 Beer-Lambert Law

The Beer-Lambert law states that the amount of energy absorbed by a solution is directly proportional to the medium's density of absorbing/scattering particles and the distance traveled through it.

## Absorption

Particles absorb light when it travels through a transmissive volume. This effect is called absorption. Because energy is absorbed, a ray loses radiance as it travels through the medium. The amount of radiance lost while travelling through the medium can be described by the medium's absorption cross section $\sigma_a$.

For an differential length of $dx$, the change in radiance $dL$ because of absorption is given by the equation

$$
dL = -\sigma_a L dx
$$

## Out-Scattering

Out-scattering is the effect describing how collisions when a ray passing through a medium may cause it to scatter. It causes radiance to be reduced while passing through the medium because some of it is deflected when scattering. Similar to absorption, the amount of radiance lost while travelling through the medium can be described by the medium's scattering coefficient $\sigma_s$.

For an differential length of $dx$, the change in radiance $dL$ because of out-scattering is given by the equation

$$
dL = -\sigma_s L dx
$$

## Attenuation

The total loss in radiance because of the combined absorption and out-scattering effect is called attenuation or extinction, denoted by $\sigma_t = \sigma_a+\sigma_s$.

For a differential length of $dx$, the change in radiance $dL$ because of attenuation is given by the equation

$$
dL = -\sigma_t L dx
$$

## Transmittance

By definition, beam transmittance describes the fraction of radiance that is transmitted between $2$ points.
We denote beam transmittance as $T_r = \dfrac{L_d}{L_0}$,
where $d$ is the total distance travelled.

We can get $T_r$ by solving the differential equation $dL = -\sigma_t L dx$:

$$
\begin{align*}
dL &= -\sigma_t L dx \\
\dfrac{dL}{L} &= -\sigma_t dx \\
\int_{L_0}^{L_d}{\dfrac{1}{L}}dL &= \int_0^d{-\sigma_t}dx \\
\ln(L_d) - \ln(L_0) &= -\int_0^d{\sigma_t}dx \\
\dfrac{L_d}{L_0} &= e^{-\int_0^d{\sigma_t}dx} \\
T_r &= e^{-\int_0^d{\sigma_t}dx}
\end{align*}
$$

Also, $\int_0^d{\sigma_t}dx$ is called the optical thickness $\tau$ between the $2$ points.
So, $T_r$ can be collapsed to

$$
T_r = e^{-\tau}
$$

In a homogenous medium, $\sigma_t$ is constant, so
$\tau = \sigma_t\int_0^d dx = \sigma_t d$ and we get the Beer-Lambert Law:

$$
T_r = e^{-\sigma_t d}
$$

## Implementation

The following only includes the Beer-Lambert section:

```glsl
float distTravelled = max(length(si.p - entryPoint), 1e-4);
vec3 absorption = -log(max(si.mat.baseCol, 1e-4));
transmittance = exp(-absorption * distTravelled);
```

Note: to fully integrate this in a path tracer, there must be checks for if the ray has completely **transmitted** through the medium to correctly implement Beer-Lambert.
