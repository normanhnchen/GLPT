# Lambert's Cosine Law

Lambert's cosine law states that the outgoing radiance $L_o$ from a reflected surface is directly proportional to the cosine of the angle $\cos{\theta}$ between the incoming direction $\omega_i$ and the surface normal $\text{n}_s$.

## Lambertian Diffuse

Because a BSDF is energy conserving, the total light reflected cannot exceed the surface albedo (base color) $\rho$. We can prove this by integrating the *constant* Lambertian BSDF $f_s$ over the hemisphere $\Omega$ with respect to the solid angle $d\omega$:

$$
\rho = \int_{\Omega}{f_s \cos{\theta}} d\omega
$$

Since $f_s$ is constant,

$$
\rho = f_s\int_{\Omega}{\cos{\theta}} d\omega
$$

The differential solid angle $d\omega$ is defined as $d\omega = \sin{\theta}d\theta d\phi$. Therefore,

$$
\begin{align*}
\rho &= f_s\int_{\Omega}{\cos{\theta}} \sin{\theta}d\theta d\phi \\
&= f_s\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2} \cos{\theta}\sin{\theta}d\theta d\phi \\
\end{align*}
$$

where the outer integral $\int_0^{2\pi}$ is with respect to the azimuthal angle $\phi$ and the inner integral $\int_0^{\pi/2}$ is with respect to the polar angle $\theta$. Thus, we can split up the integral:

$$
\begin{align*}
\rho &= f_s\int_0^{2\pi}d\phi\int_0^{\pi/2} \cos{\theta}\sin{\theta}d\theta \\
\rho &= f_s\cdot 2\pi\int_0^{\pi/2} \cos{\theta}\sin{\theta}d\theta \\
\end{align*}
$$

To solve $\int_0^{\pi/2} \cos{\theta}\sin{\theta}d\theta$, we use $u$-substituion.

Let $u = \sin{\theta}$. Therefore, $du = \cos{\theta}d\theta$. Substituting $u$ back into the original integral, we have

$$
\int_{\sin0}^{\sin(\pi/2)} u\ du = \left. \dfrac{u^2}{2} \right|_0^{1} = \dfrac{1^2}{2} - 0 = \dfrac{1}{2}
$$

Finally, we plug this result into our previous equation:

$$
\begin{align*}
\rho &= f_s\cdot 2\pi \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} \\
\rho &= f_s\cdot \pi \\
\end{align*}
$$

Therefore, for a Lambertian diffuse surface,

$$
f_s = \dfrac{\rho}{\pi}
$$
